### D1 — Correctly rounded floating-literal conversion (Lean-only seam; finding 4)
**Goal (fixed):** `CerbFloat.of_string` returns, for every well-formed C floating literal (decimal and hexadecimal, with or without one trailing `f`/`F`/`l`/`L`), the IEEE 754 binary64 value nearest to the literal's exact rational value under round-to-nearest-even, with gradual underflow to subnormals and ±0, and overflow to ±inf — the mirror of `caml_float_of_hex` (`floats.c:286-350`) and `strtod`. Malformed input keeps the existing `panic!` mirror of OCaml's `Failure`.

**Shape (guidance, not text):** parse to `(neg : Bool, m : Nat, e : Int)` with value `m · 2^e` (hex: `e = p − 4·fracDigits`) or `m · 10^e` (decimal); reduce to an exact rational `num/den` in `Nat`; if `m = 0` return the signed zero via `Float.ofBits`; otherwise compute the binade `E = ⌊log₂(num/den)⌋` exactly (bit lengths, one comparison), the target precision (53 bits, or `53 − (−1022 − E)` in the subnormal range, or overflow if `E ≥ 1024`), the truncated significand `q = ⌊num · 2^s / den⌋` at that precision, the remainder for the tie/sticky decision, round to nearest even (a carry may bump the binade), and assemble sign · biased exponent · fraction into a `UInt64` for `Float.ofBits`. Keep it in ONE total function `roundToBinary64 (neg : Bool) (num den : Nat) : Float` (den > 0) used by BOTH literal forms; no `partial`, no `Float` arithmetic before the final assembly (`Float.ofNat`/`scaleB`/`ofScientific` are the defect), no magic values beyond the IEEE constants named as such (`53`, `1023`, `1074`, `2^52`), each cited to IEEE 754-2019 §3.4 in a comment. Rewrite the module comment (`CerbFloat.lean:60-90`): what is mirrored, `file:line` cites, and the statement that any residual difference from the oracle is a bug — the "deliberate, documented divergence" wording is deleted.

**Acceptance:** (a) the audit's reproducer (`0x1` + 260 zeros + `p-1040`, compared with `1.0`) and its control are new files in `tests/float/` and MATCH the fork oracle (`Specified(1)` is the oracle's observed verdict — you are matching an observation, not predicting one).
